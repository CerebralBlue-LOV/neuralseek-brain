# Selling AI to Regulated Enterprise

**AI Salon Keynote — Run of Show**
20 minutes · 50–100 Miami founders, consultants, investors · ~10 min teach / ~10 min demo · Goal: build authority · Tone: Chamath — blunt, first-principles, numbers over narrative

---

## 0:00–1:00 — Cold open (the hook)

- Walk on with one line, no throat-clearing: *"Every enterprise AI pilot you've heard about this year — 95% of them will never reach production. Let me tell you why, and what the 5% are doing differently."*
- Frame the credibility: *"I sell AI into hospitals, global trading houses, and regulated operators. The ones who say 'no' for a living. Here's what I've learned."*

## 1:00–12:00 — TEACH: How regulated enterprise actually buys AI

**1. Lead with the scar tissue (social proof).** Three rooms, three lessons.
- *Children's Health CTO* — in healthcare, the AI conversation is a risk conversation. You don't sell a model, you sell a way to say "yes" safely.
- *Itochu Global AI* — a $100B+ trading house doesn't want a chatbot, it wants its own institutional knowledge, controlled and owned.
- *Great Day Improvements CTO* — operators don't buy "AI," they buy a number: payback, throughput, cost out.

**2. Custom AI is the only path to real ROI.** Off-the-shelf assistants demo well and die in procurement. ROI lives in *your* data, *your* process, *your* guardrails — not in a generic model someone else also rents. Generic AI is a cost center; custom AI is a P&L line.
- *"We design, build, power, and maintain custom AI around your workflows. Custom software used to mean vendor lock-in — we deliver the opposite: your business logic, your workflows, our accountability for delivery."*
- The numbers (preview the demos): **Children's Health** replaced a growing dependency on OpenAI consultants with an in-house skillset — millions saved over four years, and the compliance workflow off-the-shelf software never accommodated. **Itochu** cut analyst time on critical PowerPoints and research reports from *three weeks to thirteen minutes* — inside their firewall, model-flexible. **A manufacturer** automated 1st- and 2nd-tier HR requests so the HR team works exceptions now, not mundane repetition.

**3. The maintenance trap (why the incumbent model is rotten).** *"Most enterprise software costs more to maintain each year than it cost to build. That isn't an accident — it's the vendor's business model."* Every modernization project of the last decade ended one of two ways: 2–3x over budget and 18 months late, or killed halfway through — leaving the legacy system in production, five years older and harder to touch. The reason is always the same: *"nobody can extract the business logic from the old system. It lives in the vendor's heads, in Stack Overflow threads, in a comment from 2014 that says 'don't touch this, the regulator will sue.' The moment you try to rebuild, you realize you don't actually know what the system does."* Then: *"AI just broke that model. We turn modernization from a three-year gamble into a structured, compounding rebuild — the business keeps ownership of the logic, the codebase gets rebuilt, the knowledge survives. The maintenance era is ending. The rewrite era is starting."*

**4. How real enterprise sees Claude Code.** Be honest and specific: developers love it, but CISOs and procurement see an ungoverned, un-auditable black box touching regulated systems. *"Brilliant tool. Wrong owner. Enterprise needs the capability without surrendering the controls."*

**5. The big-consultant dynamic (the fox in the henhouse).** The Accentures of the world sell you 18 months of bodies and a slide deck — priced for the org chart, not the outcome. But the deeper mistake is structural: *"If you run a consulting business and you deploy Anthropic or OpenAI directly into your org — I'm looking at you, PwC and Accenture — you've let the fox into the henhouse. They are openly funding and launching competitors to you, while using your usage to make themselves stronger. That's not a failure on their part. It's a failure on yours."*

**6. The thesis that earns the room (contrarian peak).** OpenAI and Anthropic are not your friends — they are your *landlords*. Get you embedded, become load-bearing, then raise the rent. *"Token pricing is a honeymoon. Lock-in is the business model."*
- The winning move: *"The consulting firms that get this are adopting a control plane — they arbitrate where tokens go and who generates them. Controlling the tokens is controlling oil."*
- *"That's a core pillar of NeuralSeek's ecosystem — partnerships with 1-to-100-person agencies — and a core feature of the platform: we control token generation and direct it to any model provider. We're close to another global partnership, and we'll announce it soon."*
- Land it: *"The organizations that win refuse to accept disruption standing still — and refuse to accelerate the very companies trying to disrupt them."*

> Transition (name NeuralSeek here): *"So that's the problem. Here's the frame for the fix — the revolutionary technology of the Industrial Revolution wasn't the machines, it was the assembly line. The machine that makes the machine. That is NeuralSeek for enterprise AI. Let me show you the 5%."*

## 12:00–19:00 — DEMO: Three wins, three disciplines

Frame upfront: every regulated AI deal is won on **guardrails, governance, and strategic positioning.** One live demo per discipline — **~2:20 each, sharp hit, no wandering.** Show the one moment that proves the point, then move.

- **Children's Health → GUARDRAILS.** *We beat OpenAI for a 3-year contract.* Show how answers are constrained, sourced, and safe-by-default — the difference between "an AI said it" and "an AI can prove it."
- **Itochu → GOVERNANCE.** *We beat the well-known industry-specialist products and Claude Code.* Live demo of the **Itochu custom platform** — owned, auditable, institutional knowledge under their control, not rented.
- **GreyMatter → STRATEGIC POSITIONING.** *A Clay takeout.* Live demo — show how custom AI displaces an incumbent point tool by being the platform, not the feature.

## 19:00–20:00 — Close (claim the authority)

- *"The winners in regulated AI won't be the ones with the best model. They'll be the ones who own their AI layer — guardrailed, governed, and positioned to not get taxed for the next decade."*
- Pre-CTA line: *"If your 2026 maintenance budget is larger than your 2026 roadmap — what are you actually paying for?"*
- CTA: *"If you're sitting on a vendor contract that has slipped twice and you'd like to see what the replacement economics look like for your industry — reach out to my team. And if you're investing in this space, that's the conversation I want to have tonight."*
