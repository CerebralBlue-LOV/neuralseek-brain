# "Why Now?" — A 3-Slide Segment

*A drop-in segment for any NeuralSeek deck, built from third-party data published by a top-tier Silicon Valley venture firm in May 2026. Establishes urgency without relying on NeuralSeek's own claims.*

## Slide 1 of 3 — The Pattern Always Repeats

### Title
**Every major computing paradigm looked like a feature before it looked like infrastructure.**

### Body copy

- The **PC** looked like a productivity tool.
- The **internet** looked like a publishing medium.
- The **smartphone** looked like a better phone.
- The **cloud** looked like cheaper servers.
- **Today, AI agents look like a smarter chatbot.**

They are emerging as a new software substrate that will run workflows, services, and human-machine collaboration.

### Speaker notes

> Every one of these paradigms had a window of about 24–36 months where the leadership of the next decade got decided. Companies that recognized them as infrastructure — not features — built the platforms underneath. We are inside that window for AI agents right now.

### Source
A top-tier Silicon Valley venture firm, "A Primer on AI Agents," May 2026, p.81

## Slide 2 of 3 — The Economics Just Flipped

### Title
**Inference cost fell ~1,000× in three years. That's the unlock.**

### Body copy

| Year | Cost per million tokens (frontier-quality LLM) |
|---|---|
| 2021 (GPT-3) | ~$60 |
| 2023 (GPT-4) | ~$30 |
| 2024 (GPT-4-class via newer models) | ~$0.30 |
| 2026 (Claude Haiku, Gemini Flash, GPT-5 mini) | Pennies per million |

**Three years ago, an agent that called the model 50 times in a loop was economically unviable. Today it costs a few cents.**

That is why agentic AI is now in production — and why it wasn't possible before.

### Speaker notes

> When a model gets 1,000 times cheaper, you don't run one prompt at a time anymore. You run hundreds in a loop. That's the architectural shift from chatbot to agent. And that's what makes governance, orchestration, and cost-control the new bottleneck — because every one of those hundreds of calls is a new chance to hallucinate, leak data, or run away on token spend.

### Source
A top-tier Silicon Valley venture firm, "A Primer on AI Agents," May 2026, p.54 (sourced from a16z, Epoch.ai, OpenAI Pricing)

---

## Slide 3 of 3 — The Scale Is Coming Faster Than Anyone Realizes

### Title
**Agents proliferate per workflow, not per user. The growth is exponential, not linear.**

### Body copy

| Metric | 2025 | 2030 | CAGR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active AI agents inside enterprises | ~28.6 million | 2.216 **billion** | 139% |
| Annual tasks executed by agents | 44 billion | 415 **trillion** | 524% |

In 2030, **a single knowledge worker will run many agents simultaneously**, and those agents will spawn other agents. Each agent runs many tasks. Each task uses many tokens.

**Tasks and tokens grow significantly faster than agent count itself.**

### Speaker notes

> Notice the gap between agent-count growth and task growth. 139% vs 524%. That gap is the entire conversation about token spend, governance, and orchestration. If you're sizing your AI infrastructure for "one agent per employee," you're already wrong by an order of magnitude.

### Source
A top-tier Silicon Valley venture firm, "A Primer on AI Agents," May 2026, p.13 (sourced from IDC Report on AI Agents and Goldman Sachs)
