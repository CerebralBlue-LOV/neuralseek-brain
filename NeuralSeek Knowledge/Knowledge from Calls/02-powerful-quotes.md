> **"These pointed off-the-shelf solutions are starting to charge a lot. They're not custom. And then they're never going to fix their problems because they have thousands of customers. That's why this is like the SaaS apocalypse."**

### On the back nine vs. front nine

### On the value of customization

## Lines That Reframe the Buyer's Thinking

### When prospect says "we already have ChatGPT"

> **"It's one thing to talk about it, it's another thing to code this and be aware of it during the development cycle. Every AI go-live like Itochu's has 107 agent workflows. Each does on average four LLM calls. You have 400 to 500 queries against different LLMs in one application. Each one, how long is it? Is it grounded in truth? Are you able to look at performance over time? When the analyst gets a PDF with wrong data, can you go back and see which LLM call was the bad actor?"**

### When prospect says "we'll just build it"

> **"In the next four months, you're going to have more complex LLMs, more complex feature requests, crazier attack vectors. The project over the last four months has gotten pretty damning. And then you're going to have your people start building."**

### When prospect treats it as a chatbot project

> **"Chatbots are a fine entry point, but it's very convoluted. Anthropic, OpenAI, Copilot — they're all in there. No one's doing what we did with Itochu... It's not the story of that specific space. It's the story of incredibly custom AI."**

### When prospect says "we'll just use one LLM"

> **"What happens when Sam Altman and OpenAI crash and burn and die? Now this app is leveraging an LLM that gets deprecated. You've got to go find every place that developer plugged GPT into this app and switch it. That's not fun. That's very hard. In our solution, all of these things are two clicks."**
