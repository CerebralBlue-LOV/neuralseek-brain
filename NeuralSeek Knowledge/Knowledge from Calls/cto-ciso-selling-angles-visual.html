Angle 01 · AI Advisement

Sell trusted-advisor positioning.

The CTO/CISO is drowning in AI pitches. Their internal engineers, their consultants, and their LLM vendors all have a financial reason to mislead them. Earn the room by being the only party that doesn't.

Buyer's private fear
?
"I don't know who to trust."
Internal engineers protect headcount. Consultants take kickbacks from whichever LLM is paying that quarter. LLM vendors push their own model into the stack so deep it can't be swapped. Every voice is conflicted.
The reframe
Every party in the room is conflicted — except the LLM-agnostic platform.
NeuralSeek runs every major LLM. We have zero financial incentive to push you toward one provider. That's why CTOs at regulated enterprises text us as advisors, not as customers.

Sell career-safe, reversible architecture.

The CTO/CISO isn't just managing technology risk — they're managing career risk. The story that earns the deal tells them how they will still be employed in 2029 because of the architecture they picked today.

Angle 03 · Knowledge Protection

Sell the protection of the institutional moat.

The deepest fear isn't "AI fails." It's "AI succeeds, the vendor owns the implementation, and our company's competitive moat now lives on someone else's server with someone else's renewal terms."

Buyer's private fear
"Our institutional knowledge is migrating to our vendors."
Business logic ends up in the consultant's Notion. In a Stack Overflow thread. In a comment from 2014 that says "don't touch this, the regulator will sue." The next upgrade — they own it. You rent it back forever.
The reframe
Your Firewall
Custom AI either protects your knowledge or hands it to the consultant.
Children's Health replaced a growing dependency on OpenAI consultants with in-house skillsets — every line of logic stayed at Children's Health. Itochu's workflows live on-prem, editable by their team, inside their firewall.
